# Wrestler Builds

Your **build** is how your wrestler is put together: a fighting style, a match specialty, four stats and the levels of your signatures and finishers. You pay for it with **build points**, and how many you get depends on your division. When a GM settles a match with the match sim, your build is what goes into the ring.

## Where to find it

Open your profile and look for the **Build** card on the **Overview** tab. Choose **Set up build** the first time, or **Edit build** after that. Your dashboard also reminds you when you have unspent points, when your build is over budget, or when you haven't picked a specialty yet.

## Build points per division

Higher divisions get bigger budgets:

| Division | Build points |
|----------|--------------|
| **Heavyweight** (tier 1) | 160 |
| **Mid Card** (tier 2) | 130 |
| **Jobber** (tier 3) | 100 |
| **Young Boys** | 80 |
| No division | 80 |

The meter at the top of the editor shows what you've spent, your budget and what's left. You can't save a build that costs more than your budget.

## Stats and what they cost

There are four stats: **Strikes**, **Grapple**, **Agility** and **Endurance**. Every stat starts at 40 and caps at 100. The higher a stat gets, the more each extra point costs:

| Stat value | Cost per point |
|------------|----------------|
| 41 to 75 | 1 |
| 76 to 90 | 2 |
| 91 to 100 | 3 |

So raising one stat from 40 to 75 costs 35 points, and taking it all the way to 100 costs 95. The editor shows the cost of the next point next to each slider and stops you when you run out.

Which stats matter most depends on the match. A ladder match leans on Agility, a submission match on Grapple, an iron man match on Endurance, a street fight on Strikes, and a cage match on Strikes and Grapple. A standard match weighs all four, with Strikes and Grapple a little ahead.

## Styles

Your style gives you a free stat bonus and decides who you match up well against:

| Style | Bonus | Strong against | Weak against |
|-------|-------|----------------|--------------|
| **Powerhouse** | +8 Grapple | High-flyer | Striker |
| **High-flyer** | +8 Agility | Technician | Powerhouse |
| **Technician** | +4 Grapple, +4 Endurance | Brawler | High-flyer |
| **Brawler** | +4 Strikes, +4 Endurance | Striker | Technician |
| **Striker** | +8 Strikes | Powerhouse | Brawler |
| **All-rounder** | +2 to every stat | No edge or weakness | No edge or weakness |

The five styles form a circle: Powerhouse beats High-flyer, High-flyer beats Technician, Technician beats Brawler, Brawler beats Striker, and Striker beats Powerhouse. Being on the right side of a matchup is a small edge, not a guaranteed win. Between two otherwise equal wrestlers it's roughly a 57 to 43 split. The All-rounder sits outside the circle.

The bonus is added on top of the points you buy and still caps at 100. It's free and doesn't count against your budget.

## Your match specialty

Pick one kind of match you're best at. When a match falls in your specialty, you get a bonus in the sim. The specialty is free, but it has to fit your style:

| Style | Specialties it can pick |
|-------|-------------------------|
| **Powerhouse** | Hardcore, Cage |
| **High-flyer** | High risk, Cage |
| **Technician** | Technical, Endurance |
| **Brawler** | Hardcore, Endurance |
| **Striker** | Hardcore, Endurance |
| **All-rounder** | Any of them, with half the bonus |

What each kind of match covers:

- **High risk**: Ladder, TLC, Scaffold
- **Technical**: Submission, I Quit, 2 out of 3 Falls
- **Endurance**: Iron Man, Last Man Standing
- **Hardcore**: Street Fight, Extreme Rules, Tables, Falls Count Anywhere
- **Cage**: Steel Cage, Hell in a Cell

So a big Powerhouse can't specialize in ladder matches. The editor greys out the specialties your style can't take and tells you why. If you switch to a style that doesn't allow your current specialty, the editor clears it and asks you to pick another one. Standard matches never count as a specialty.

## Move levels

Every signature and finisher in your moveset has a level from 1 to 5. Level 1 is free. Each level above that costs:

- **Signature**: 5 points per level
- **Finisher**: 8 points per level

Your best finisher counts the most, then the average of your signatures. Higher levels make you a little stronger in the sim, and the play-by-play names your real moves. Add moves to your moveset first (see [My Profile](profile)), then level them up in the build editor. Removing a move gives its points back.

## Re-spending after a move

Your budget always comes from the division you're in right now, so a division move can change it. See [Promotion and Relegation](division-movement) for how moves happen.

- **Moving up** gives you more points. Your dashboard shows how many are unspent.
- **Moving down** can leave your build over your new budget. You get a notification saying by how much, and the editor shows **Over budget** in red. Tap the notification to go straight to the editor.

The move itself is never blocked. Until you re-spend, matches the GMs simulate use a scaled-down copy of your build: the points you bought above 40 and your move levels above 1 all shrink by the same share until the build fits your new budget. Your saved build isn't changed, so lower whatever you like, save, and you're back to full strength.

## The build card

Once you've saved a build, everyone can see it on your profile: your style, your specialty ("Specialist: Hardcore"), four stat bars and your move levels. The bars show the stats the sim uses, with the style bonus included and marked. If you're over budget, that's the scaled-down copy, so the card shows what you'd really bring to a match today.

Until you save a build, you use the default: All-rounder, every stat at 40, no specialty. The default build stays off your public profile, and only you see the **Set up your build** prompt.

## Simulated matches

The GMs can settle a scheduled match with the match sim instead of playing it. A simulated result counts like any other: records, standings and titles all update. It shows a **Simulated** badge, and anyone can open the play-by-play to see how it went. Win chances and previews are only for the GMs, so neither you nor your opponent sees the odds.

Better stats, higher move levels, good recent form, a favourable style matchup and fighting in your specialty all help. None of them guarantees a win, and upsets happen.
